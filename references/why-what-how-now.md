# The Why / What / How / Now Matrix

The lesson structure behind every workshop, lesson, or YouTube video. Four quadrants, no axes — just the flow of the lesson. Everything you ever teach is a list: a hook, what you're going to cover, and 3–5 key chapters.

## Why — the Heart (10–15 slides, ~10–15 min)

The emotional buy-in. Why should they care? Why lean in for the next 45 minutes?

- Open with a personal anecdote, a status-quo challenge, or a before/after.
- This creates the emotional connection between speaker and audience the moment they join.
- Delivery bonus: the Why is also the speaker's warm-up. The first 15 minutes of any live call are rough — the Why is low-stakes runway to find rhythm.
- Example (Workshop Mastery itself): teacher vs. coach (Mr. Coghlan story), live education is future-proof against AI, your program is a living curriculum.

## What — the Brain (5–10 slides)

The specific outcome. The cognitive side of the intro: "not only do I want this, I now *need* this."

- Literally a checklist: "Here's what you're getting today" — items one through six.
- Frame as capability: what will they be able to DO after this that they couldn't before?
- Short and punchy. One item per slide works.

## How — the Body (60–80+ slides)

The meat and potatoes. The systems, frameworks, and processes. The entire value of the lesson.

- 3–5 chapters (7 absolute max).
- For EVERY chapter: explain what you're teaching AND attach it to a metaphor, story, or visual. (Matrix diagram for structure; Alex Hormozi's 1,250-slides-in-90-minutes story for slide philosophy; the software/hardware flowchart for tech.)
- Re-engage between chapters: stop, ask "how's everyone feeling? thumbs up?" — it re-engages the room and gives the speaker a beat to reset.

## Now — the Action (5–10 slides)

What do they do immediately after? Learning Why/What/How isn't enough — they need to DO something to make the information utilized.

- Give a tangible walk-away: a worksheet, a prompt, an AI workflow, a tool, a Google Sheet.
- The Now asset is built in Phase 4, but it's named on slide one of this section.

## Slide Philosophy

- **Cue cards, not a presentation.** The speaker is scripting the lesson in slide form, then riffing between the cards. Words/visuals on screen that spark thoughts and keep them on track.
- **150–200 slides per 45–60 minute lesson** = a new slide every 10–15 seconds. More slides = more engagement; every slide change is a visual re-engagement point (the audience thinks "new thing — better take notes").
- **Don't think until you've got 150 slides.** Volume first. Don't worry about rhythm or flow on the first pass — just make them.
- Lessons look boring before the visual pass: white slides, black text, nothing else. Spice comes AFTER the full outline exists.
- Breaking a paragraph of outline into slides is mechanical: each dot point becomes a slide. That's why 150 isn't scary.
- Prepared-with-many-slides means never worrying about going UNDER time — only over.
- Tooling is irrelevant (Figma, Keynote, Google Slides, Canva, Gamma, PowerPoint). This skill asks each user which platform they want during onboarding — see `slide-platforms.md`.

## Slide Count Budget (use when generating deck.json)

For a 45–60 min lesson targeting ~170 slides:

| Section | Slides |
|---|---|
| Cover + housekeeping | 2–3 |
| Why | 12–15 |
| What | 6–10 |
| How — per chapter (×3–5) | 15–22 each |
| Chapter dividers + re-engage beats | 1–2 each |
| Now | 6–10 |
| Close / Q&A pre-frame | 2–3 |
